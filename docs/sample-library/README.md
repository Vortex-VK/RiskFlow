# Sample library

Choose **Samples** in the editing or playback toolbar. The 250-payment example
is selected initially. Open it and select **Simulate**, or download the same CSV.

## Engine evaluation panel

**Model evaluation scope** shows a separate development evaluation of the installed
Stack-d5 head, not the curated samples or the Auto queue. The compact
[evaluation data](../../frontend/src/features/samples/evaluation.json) records all five
source populations, counts, the installed head hash and original receipt hash.
It can also be downloaded from the panel. No transaction records are included.

The source is the recorded `nonlinear-stack-v2/reports/source-metrics.csv`, filtered
to `cohort=all`, `policy=batch`, `budget=0.05`, model `stack_d5`.
The 484,663 payments comprise IBM TabFormer, Sparkov, Fraud Detection Handbook,
Household simulation and BankSim. Each dataset reviews its top 5%, rounded up.
Mean fraud recall gives each dataset equal weight: **80.1%** for Stack-d5.
Per-source detections and false alerts are available under Results by dataset;
the headline is not pooled accuracy or review precision.

The panel leads with two user-facing benefits: the measured fraud capture at a
limited review budget, and inspectable point contributions explaining a payment's
priority. A short engine description explains how eight specialist models feed
a final combining model with 33 history measures, and how Auto suggests a queue.
Technical capacity and package details stay in the downloadable data rather than
being presented as evidence of detection quality.

The packaged scoring bundle occupies **8,896,668 bytes (8.9 MB decimal)**: nine
CatBoost models, reference scales and the manifest. File sizes are recorded in the
snapshot. This excludes application code, runtime dependencies and in-memory
transaction data. Inference uses CPU and does not require a GPU or external model
API; this is not a comparative latency or memory benchmark.

Explainability is quantified by **two levels of checked arithmetic**, displayed
at **0.1-point precision**: observation contributions sum to each expanded check,
and check contributions sum to the centered priority score. Remaining context is
included explicitly. These are reconstruction guarantees for displayed results,
not a percentage of human comprehension or proof of fraud. See the
[calculation method](../stack-d5-integration/review-controls.md).
The downloaded evaluation data includes these definitions and current limits.

The model hash matches the locked research candidate and installed bundle.
These sources were exposed during development, so this is not independent
validation, an industry comparison or an estimate for arbitrary uploads.

## Sources and selection

The five-payment IBM walkthrough remains unchanged, with its
[original attribution](../../frontend/public/samples/ibm/ATTRIBUTION.txt).
The other five examples are generated by
[sample_scenarios.py](../../scripts/sample_scenarios.py). Accounts, merchants,
amounts, timestamps and labels are fictional. No private user uploads or source
training rows were copied. The generated CSV data are CC0; see
[scenario notes](../../frontend/public/samples/scenarios/README.txt) and
[attribution](../../frontend/public/samples/scenarios/ATTRIBUTION.txt).

The previous selection optimized detection results among limited public-source
windows without assessing graph appearance. Its 250-payment sample had two accounts
and 205 merchants. That topology produced sparse, repetitive graphs.

This replacement compared **237 candidates**: 200 authored ledgers (five families,
eight seeds and five sizes), 32 broader Sparkov cohort windows and five retained
IBM samples. The authored families vary shared shopping, exploration, everyday
categories and payment-method habits. Their generator never reads model outputs.
Every complete candidate is scored from its own visible history, with no hidden
warmup, fraud feedback, row removal or relabeling. Whole-candidate selection uses
F1 (balancing precision and recall), followed by rendered graph inspection.
All candidate results, selected seeds and the prior catalog are recorded in the
[selection receipt](scenario-selection-2026-10-29.json).

The chosen family assumes 3.5% of ordinary purchases are online, with fraud
incidents occurring online. Legitimate large and unfamiliar purchases and smaller
fraudulent purchases are included. This specific, favorable setting should not be
mistaken for a general-purpose accuracy result. The other tested families allow
16% or 36% ordinary online purchases; they generated more false alerts.

| Payments | Accounts | Merchants | Shared merchants | Repeat account-to-merchant payments |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 | 6 | 14 | 8 | 44.0% |

| 5,000 | 180 | 363 | 290 | 67.6% |
| 10,000 | 340 | 687 | 553 | 68.0% |

A shared merchant serves more than one account; repeat share counts payments after
the first visit by that account to that merchant. Actual app rendering was reviewed
at all five sizes. The larger scenarios form connected shopping communities rather
than a handful of accounts surrounded by mostly one-time merchant visits.

The current selection receipt retains the previous catalog and comparison metrics.
Superseded Sparkov downloads and daily-policy reports have been removed. Broader
public cohorts did not improve either repeat-visit structure or review precision.
Lower false-alert counts in some legacy IBM samples came with substantially more
missed fraud, so those were not chosen.

## Reproduction

[Catalog](../../frontend/src/features/samples/catalog.json) and
[current score receipts](stack-d5-auto/catalog.json) record the active automatic
queue. Per-sample receipt files contain raw scores, flags and original labels;
`sources/` maps each result to its generated CSV row or retained IBM source row.
Browser tests compare live imported model inputs, scores and the UI queue with the
receipts, including scoring with labels removed.

```powershell
$env:PYTHONPATH = 'backend'
# Reproduce all 200 authored candidates without changing published samples:
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/compare_sample_scenarios.py
# Rebuild the selected CSVs and receipts (checks the recorded generator hash):
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/export_sample_scenarios.py
# Or verify existing CSV hashes and recompute only their scores:
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/refresh_samples.py
```

All five authored sizes fit the existing limits. The largest remains 10,000
payments; no import, account, object or file-size limits changed. Historical IBM
fixtures and their source lineage are preserved under
[`fixtures/samples/ibm/`](../../fixtures/samples/ibm/README.md) for compatibility tests.
