# V4: information and training-data experiment

Freeze July–December 2018 reserved-user outcomes as confirmation. Existing ledgers show v1 reserved evaluation in June 2018 and v2 reserved evaluation in 2019; v3 used 2019 validation-role users. None evaluated the selected reserved 2018H2 outcomes. Earlier/later data and validation users informed the hypotheses, so this is a retrospective historical holdout, not a new future-year or entirely untouched-person claim. The original membership remains fixed.

All fitting uses fit-role users before July 2018. Screening uses validation-role users during July–December 2018. Short fits use January–June 2018; broader fits use January 2016–June 2018. Extraction begins October 2015 for history. Every feature uses strictly earlier observations available by its target time. Context features may observe unlabelled histories across user groups, mirroring a population-wide transaction feed, but never their labels. All methods receive identical target transactions, chronological history and evaluation eligibility.

The fixed experiments change one component at a time: account sequence/timing features, merchant activity across accounts, or broader training with the original feature set. EBM parameters remain at the v2 incumbent configuration. Standard CatBoost remains a comparator. A sequence-plus-merchant experiment is permitted only if both separate variants gain at least two recall points without AP regression under both pilot seeds. No arbitrary feature search follows a failed screening or confirmation result.

Pilot fitting preserves every fraud example and hash-samples legitimate purchases toward 100,000 negatives per fit window. Inverse inclusion weights restore their training contribution. Histories are generated from all transactions before sampling; no label affects historical features. Sampling uses a fixed event-content hash, independent of models and labels within the negative class. All short-period methods use identical pilot rows. Final short-period models use all eligible rows. The broad method retains all positives and samples toward 600,000 negatives; if selected, its claim concerns the broader weighted training procedure, not a pure causal attribution to duration alone.

One nominee advances only if it gains at least two pooled recall points and does not regress AP under either seed. Eligible candidates rank by mean quarterly recall, worst quarterly recall and mean quarterly AP. Selection is exploratory and freezes before full fitting. Confirmation requires at least five recall points over both refitted comparators, positive paired 97.5% user-bootstrap lower bounds, no AP/coverage point regression and 100 frauds across 20 users. The two intervals allocate a family-wise 5% error budget; use 2,000 replicates and recompute top-K. Month-block sensitivity and 2%/5% budgets cannot replace the primary endpoint. No winner swapping or test enlargement after labels open.

Power planning from the consumed v3 paired interval gives an approximate standard error of 2.41 points with 28 fraud-bearing users. Under unchanged variance and independent-user scaling, a five-point gain would need about 62 fraud-bearing users for 80% power at the corrected level; eight points would need about 24. This is a planning approximation, not a guarantee for this cohort or a reason to inspect its outcomes early. Weak support or a wide interval must be reported as inconclusive. A materially large feature gain is the target; more rows cannot manufacture one.

Development diagnosis starts from consumed v3 pilot outcomes: the incumbent missed 147/217 frauds; the 217 frauds involve 46 merchants, 33 shared by multiple fraud-bearing users, and 25 accounts. Most account-hour groups contain a single fraud (173 groups), with 19 containing two and two containing three. This motivates, but does not prove, relational and sequence hypotheses. Popular merchants can naturally occur across many users; shared identity alone is not evidence of wrongdoing. No fraud-labelled merchant reputation is constructed.

New measurements cover five-minute attempts, one-hour merchant/category diversity and failure history, changes relative to the preceding attempt/completed amount, and personal hour frequency. Merchant measurements cover one-hour/seven-day activity and distinct accounts, recent failure fraction, activity-rate change and completed-amount deviation. Geography, inferred devices, current errors/status and label-derived merchant risk remain excluded. Unknown or weak context is explicit. Larger models and raw merchant-ID memorization are not part of this experiment.

The source is synthetic and the supported task remains positive standalone USD purchase attempts. Merchant features require access to the broader observed feed; they cannot be silently reproduced from an isolated one-account upload. Native explanation arithmetic, immutable prediction/report replay and context-invariance tests are required. A statistical success nominates research software for separately scoped application integration; existing app behavior is unchanged. Preserve all v1–v3 sources, artifacts, `plan.md` and `todo.md`.
