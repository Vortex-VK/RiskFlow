# V5: precision at a minimum recall

This new objective follows the user's request to improve precision. The v4 result remains negative under its original top-1% recall objective. Post-hoc exploration of its consumed development predictions suggested that sequence features could improve the upper part of the ranking. This is hypothesis generation, not a validated precision upgrade.

Use the existing v4 caches and original role memberships. Fit January–June 2018 fit-role purchases, select cutoffs on July–December 2018 validation-role purchases, and confirm once on reserved users in the same latter period. Earlier/later evidence already influenced development; this is a retrospective user-held-out evaluation. Do not call it a new future-year, untouched-person or external-source test. No v4 reserved evaluation outcomes have been opened. The shared context cache contains earlier unlabelled histories, but this experiment uses only account-local baseline and sequence features.

Fit the unchanged incumbent and sequence EBM configurations on every eligible fitting row, with unit weights. Each method is the mean of two raw scores from seeds 20261024 and 20261025. This symmetrical ensemble reduces dependence on choosing a lucky seed. It differs from the sampled v4 pilots, so those pilot scores cannot supply final cutoffs. Refit both methods, select their own cutoffs, freeze the models and never refit after cutoff selection.

For each method maximize development precision subject to pooled recall at least 40%. Thresholds include complete equal-score groups and alert iff raw score is at least the cutoff. Break identical-precision ties by higher recall then higher cutoff. These are fixed score decisions, not fixed alert quotas or calibrated probability cutoffs. The realized workload and recall can shift on new data. Report the full precision/recall tradeoff separately.

Advance the sequence candidate only if its ensemble precision improves by at least two percentage points, each individually thresholded seed has nonnegative precision gain, ensemble recall falls by no more than five points, and its frozen ensemble cutoff has at least 25% recall in each development quarter. This modest quarterly guard is not a claim of 40% per-quarter recall. The primary target is pooled recall. If screening fails, keep confirmation closed and predeclare any bounded hard-negative follow-up separately before fitting it. No new candidate may replace a failed model after confirmation labels open.

Freeze all source, protocol/design, cache manifests, serialized models, development predictions and thresholds before generating label-free confirmation scores. Hash confirmation scores and alerts before joining outcomes. Confirmation requires at least 100 frauds across 20 users, at least 100 alerts per method, full score coverage, both observed recalls at least 40%, candidate precision gain at least three percentage points with a positive paired-user 95% lower bound, and a paired recall-difference 95% lower bound greater than minus five points. All conditions must pass for one prespecified comparison; this intersection does not pick among multiple winners. Report individual precision/recall confidence intervals. Passing the observed recall floor does not guarantee 40% population recall; state uncertainty explicitly.

The paired bootstrap resamples users with all their rows together and keeps the fixed thresholds. It must not rerank a top-K quota or reselect thresholds within test resamples. Use 4,000 replicates, seed 20261027; undefined precision/recall replicates are counted and reported, with at least 95% defined required for the paired endpoints. Month-cluster intervals are descriptive sensitivity with only six blocks, not a replacement for the primary user-cluster gate. Fixed-decision confusion counts can be aggregated per user to make this calculation efficient and exactly equivalent to row multiplicities.

Keep v1–v4 source/artifacts and `plan.md`/`todo.md` unchanged. Record new label accesses under v5. Use two threads and the existing 2.5 GiB process budget. Test tie boundaries, absent positives, recall constraints, independent confusion counts, paired resampling and frozen label access.
