# Modeling contract

The six-rule section below describes the preserved V1 simulation. Explicit saved datasets add a [V2 retained-history envelope](retained-history.md), reviewed source revisions, lifecycle populations and frozen results. V2 uses investigation recommendations and separately reports unavailable signals; it does not reinterpret rule points as fraud probabilities or real authorization decisions. Its lifecycle adapter supplies actual attempts to V1: explicit captures, refunds, reversals and status updates are not additional attempts. Completed purchase/capture amounts and linked adjustments form separate accounting populations by currency.

The optional `as_known` mode reconstructs a source-availability snapshot at a single cutoff, bounded by a retained dataset revision. All retained versions must supply precise source availability and event times. Scores analyze this snapshot; they are not a per-authorization backtest. The retrospective mode selects the latest accepted version of each event. New corrections never modify frozen earlier runs. New saved assessments calculate [historical profiles](historical-profiles.md) from earlier completed spending, with explicit cold starts and provenance. The V1 formulas below retain their authored simulation contract; profiled V2 assessments use per-event amount/category/location context and observed device familiarity. Scores remain uncalibrated rule points.

Bundled examples and evaluation fixtures are synthetic. A rule-based risk score is not a fraud probability. Historical Stage 0 prototype expectations are not API results; current regression fixtures verify the executable engine.

The [Phase 4 outcome benchmark](simulator-evaluation/phase4/report.md) independently checks calculations and reports detection, false-alert workload, coverage and uncertainty on reserved synthetic accounts. Its poor precision is an explicit limitation. The subsequent [merchant-range evaluation](simulator-evaluation/purchase-v2/report.md) uses fresh accounts and supports a smaller false-alert reduction with unchanged detections on that cohort; most fraud labels remain missed. See the [validation contract](engine-validation.md) for separate outcomes, frozen protocols, references, incident proxies and reproducibility; this does not validate operational fraud detection.

## Six rules

| Rule | Condition | Points |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Unusual amount | At least 6 × the account baseline; otherwise at least 3 × | 45 / 30 |
| Payment velocity | At least 5 attempts in five minutes; otherwise at least 3 | 35 / 25 |
| New device | No effective familiarity time for this account/device relationship | 20 |
| Unusual location | Outside usual countries and the specific account/location relationship is unfamiliar | 20 |
| Category deviation | Payment category (falling back to merchant category) is outside the account's usual categories | 10 |
| Shared device | At least three distinct accounts actually use the device in five minutes | 25 |

The amount and velocity tiers are mutually exclusive. Sum raw points, then cap at 100. Default decisions are Approve below 40, Review at 40–69, and Block at 70–100. Review is pending; it is neither caught fraud nor approved fraud.

## Time and context

Order events by canonical UTC timestamp and ASCII event ID. Each five-minute window includes its exact lower boundary, earlier scheduled attempts, and the current attempt. All decisions count toward future history. Future events never contribute, and earlier results are not retroactively rescored.

Associations establish when context becomes usable and, separately, familiar. A payment must explicitly reference one device and one location with effective relationships. Familiarity belongs to the relationship. Drawn device links alone do not trigger sharing.

Labels, layout, and display names do not enter scoring. Thresholds affect decisions, not features or scores. Cost assumptions affect evaluation, not scores.

## Hero arithmetic

H10 is a $240 electronics purchase with a $50 typical amount. Expected points are 30 amount + 20 new device + 10 category + 25 shared device = 85. Its account velocity is only two attempts. The sharing evidence is H07/A, H08/B, H09/C, and H10/A.

Changing H09 to C’s existing familiar phone leaves its synthetic fraud label unchanged. H09 becomes 40. H10/H11 become 60; H12/H13 become 55. The C/shared association remains present but does not create an actual use. All five previous blocks become reviews.

## Evaluation vocabulary

Attempted amount is the sum of attempted purchases, not settled spend. Decision counts and amounts must reconcile to all attempts. Truth-dependent metrics use only labeled events and show coverage. Zero-denominator rates are unavailable, never zero percent.

Blocked synthetic fraud, approved synthetic fraud, and fraud pending review are distinct. Legitimate blocks are false positives under the author’s labels. Labels are assertions about a synthetic story, not validated outcomes.

Default illustrative labeled cost is approved labeled fraud amount × 100%, plus $3 for each labeled review, plus $15 for each labeled legitimate block. Round the aggregate loss half up in integer cents. The model excludes pending-fraud loss, reviewer outcomes, chargebacks, revenue, and downstream effects. Show all-payment review operating cost separately for partially labeled scenarios.

In the fully labeled hero, nine legitimate attempts total $326 and seven fraudulent attempts total $1,200. Before the edit, $840 is blocked and $360 is pending; after, all $1,200 is pending. The $6 → $21 cost change comes from two → seven reviews.

## Incomplete transaction exports

Imported payment records can carry `unavailableRules`. This marks rules whose required inputs were not supplied; it does not estimate those inputs. The engine emits `available: false`, zero points, empty measured evidence, and null corresponding features. A date-only import excludes five-minute velocity and device-sharing across its entire population, even if some rows have times, to avoid drawing time-window conclusions from partial chronology. Missing devices/locations are separate per-account graph placeholders and do not contribute device/location evidence. Unknown baselines/categories can be enabled only by explicit user opt-in to displayed model assumptions during import.

The remaining score uses the unchanged v1 thresholds and point tiers. It is a partial-evidence simulation result, not a fraud probability or finding that the payment is safe. The UI labels limited data in replay, payment explanations, event cards and totals. Existing complete templates retain all rules and their independently authored expected decisions. Available amount/time/context inputs and explicit truth labels retain their original semantics.

## Idle surface motion

Stage 6 adds two radial waves to each 48-point rendered surface, with amplitudes up to 5.5% and 2.8% of its radius and an ID-derived phase. Following the user's clarification, the main wave gently rocks back and forth over about five seconds, while a weaker traveling ripple varies its amplitude. The latest tuning increases deformation by about 73% to make the shape change more visible, with all timing unchanged. The phase spreads adjacent IDs apart so neighboring objects do not wobble in unison. Their two- and three-lobe shapes preserve the category silhouette; centroid correction and area normalization prevent drift or inflation. Account surface displacement is at most roughly 2.7 canvas units at rest. Centers, icons, labels and connection geometry are unchanged.

The idle waves are added at rendering time to the existing drag spring displacement. They do not enter simulation inputs, document positions, undo history or scoring. Resting surfaces update at up to 30 Hz; active drag springs use animation frames and bounded integration steps. Cached body references avoid repeated per-node DOM searches, and memoized glyphs avoid rebuilding resting paths during replay renders. Each graph/tray owns and cleans up its controller.

The Animate objects preference and system reduced-motion setting restore the exact rest outline and stop scheduled animation. Hidden-page handling cancels an active drag, restores rest, and suspends frames; visibility restores the idle loop. This is an appearance approximation, not material physics or risk evidence. Performance measurements describe the tested local desktop and browser, not a general frame-rate guarantee.


## Hierarchical exploration and larger imports

The rendering hierarchy is a navigation aid, not an additional fraud rule or a claim that accounts belong to the same person. Each payment belongs to exactly one leaf. An entity may appear in several leaves under its original ID. Editing that entity still edits the full scenario; opening a group never submits a filtered scenario for analysis.

For multiple accounts, profiles contain the distinct merchants and identified devices actually used by their payments. For a feature used by f of N accounts, a shared device contributes 8/f and a shared merchant contributes (1 - f/N)/f. The sum is normalized by the geometric mean of the two profile sizes. Unknown device placeholders do not establish affinity, and a merchant used by every account provides no distinguishing evidence. Repeated payments do not increase feature weights.

The resulting weighted account graph is partitioned using deterministic greedy modularity at resolution 1. Starting with individual accounts, the algorithm repeatedly selects the merge with the largest positive modularity gain, comparing observed connections against a baseline based on each account's total connection weight. It stops when no merge improves modularity; there is no absolute affinity cutoff or requested sector count. The objective is Q = sum over communities of [internal edge weight / total edge weight - (community weighted degree / (2 * total edge weight)) squared], following the [weighted modularity definition](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.community.quality.modularity.html). This is a greedy approximation, not a guarantee of the global optimum. Stable account ordering resolves equal-score candidates. Feature frequencies and the merge tree are computed once for the full scenario and retained throughout navigation, so zooming does not strengthen a popular merchant's evidence.

Sector count and membership follow the evidence, with no sector cap, volume balancing, or containers that combine independent communities for browsing. A large detected community remains one sector regardless of its share of total payments; a connected graph may contain several communities. Entering a community reveals subgroups only when their average internal pair affinity is at least 1.5 times the parent's average internal pair affinity, including unconnected pairs as zero. Weaker intermediate merges and equal-score ties are flattened to their stronger descendants or individual accounts, so arbitrary binary merge order does not imply meaningful subcommunities. A dataset can have one community or many isolated accounts. Payment volume does not affect account affinity or set quotas. These deterministic choices are heuristics for shared activity, not learned classifications, ownership claims, or fraud labels. Every payment remains present exactly once.

Community names summarize the categories of connections that distinguish whole account communities. They reuse the account profiles and feature weights used for partitioning. For each shared identity, normalized internal pair weights are summed and multiplied by max(0, 1 - outside account usage rate / inside account usage rate). This concentration score discounts identities used equally everywhere. It is an explanatory heuristic, not an exact causal attribution of the modularity objective. With no outside accounts, internal contributions are used directly.

Merchant contributions are aggregated by their recorded source category, falling back to the payment category and then merchant metadata. Whitespace, case and equivalent category labels such as restaurants/dining are normalized. A merchant with genuinely conflicting categories contributes to a mixed bucket; its entire weight is never duplicated across categories or assigned to a guessed business type. Mixed and unknown categories stay in the evidence denominator but cannot supply a specific name. Devices contribute to a separate shared-device factor. Repeated purchases do not increase identity contributions. Raw merchant or device names appear as supporting examples in hover details, rather than naming the whole community.

Sibling sectors are named together. Each factor's share of a sector's distinguishing connection evidence is compared with its share of the pooled evidence in the other sectors at that level. A factor must supply at least 15% locally, exceed the comparison by at least 10 percentage points and by a factor of 1.5, and substantially represent at least 60% of the sector's accounts. An account counts toward that coverage only when the factor supplies at least 10% of its own incident distinguishing connection evidence. This prevents a few strongly connected accounts or many negligible links from determining a sector's name. When other sectors have no comparable connection evidence, at least 50% local evidence and the same coverage requirement are necessary; hover details explicitly say that no comparison is available.

Eligible factors are ranked by their difference from peers, with up to three shown. One merchant category yields names such as `Retail-linked accounts`; multiple factors use compact labels such as `Retail links + Dining links`. These describe connection evidence, not spending proportions. If no category qualifies, the label stays broad, such as `Mixed merchant links` or `Shared devices`. Generic families must also cover 60% of accounts and supply 25% of the connection evidence; universally shared background features cannot override the identities that distinguish the community. When no distinguishing evidence exists, generic labels can describe the raw shared identities. Duplicate sibling names keep small sector references, since separate merchant networks can belong to the same category. The hover and accessible description report account coverage, evidence shares here and in peer sectors, and contributing identities. Single-account and deeper factual subdivisions retain their existing labels. Naming does not affect grouping, payment membership, connection rendering, or the account-level category breakdown.

Inside a single account, subdivisions use actual payment categories (falling back to merchant categories), merchants, recorded devices, recorded locations, and UTC calendar dates, in that order when the field has multiple values. Unknown context objects indicate missing data, not observed shared context. Groups of 30 or fewer payments open directly in detail; this is an early stopping threshold, not a maximum leaf size. Larger groups with no further distinguishing facts remain intact in detail, with aggregated routes and searchable payment lists. No equal-sized time windows or transaction-ID batches are invented. Calendar-date groups describe recorded dates, not inferred coordinated behaviour.

The overview shows every leaf object instance on one continuous map. Individual account-to-merchant routes are drawn only in final interactive detail, so their dense webs do not crowd the level above. An object used in multiple leaves appears in each relevant territory, preserving its original identity. Short and deep branches both resolve to their underlying points. Each route aggregates its actual payment count. Final views retain the interactive object physics and category colours; unconnected editor objects remain available there.

Territories are presentation geometry, separate from transaction grouping. Existing relationship-based leaf layouts supply the real object positions. Sibling sectors settle under attraction from shared identities and separation of their occupied convex hulls, with a small gap. Levels with more than 48 sectors use enclosing circles for collision separation and the strongest three links per sector on average for layout attraction, avoiding expensive hull projections for every pair while retaining every sector and its evidence. A deterministic spiral seeds the solver, then the resulting footprint is oriented along its principal horizontal axis. Labels too small to fit remain accessible on hover or keyboard focus and become visible on zoom. No prescribed enclosing shape or outer border is drawn. Sector colour distinguishes neighbouring groups without drawn borders or separators; padded hulls serve collision and hit testing only. Coordinates are an abstract layout, not geography or analytical evidence. Boundaries remain fixed; sectors cannot be individually dragged or squished. Dragging pans the camera and a movement threshold prevents a pan from opening a sector.

Inter-sector connections represent shared identified accounts, merchants or devices, not payments from one sector to another. Unknown context placeholders and locations do not establish these links. Strength weights devices by eight, accounts by four and merchants by one, discounts identities used by many siblings, and normalizes by the geometric mean of group object counts. The visible budget grows from roughly 1.2 links per sibling at the root to two at the next level and three deeper down. When all linked groups are final leaves, it is capped at one link per sibling to prevent an almost complete mesh from repeated identities. Strong incident links keep connected groups represented; remaining slots take the strongest links. Muted blue-grey, slate and faint grey distinguish relative strength. One immediate child level receives a faint preview of its own bounded shared-object links at 0.085 opacity, below the weakest current-level link. Preview links have no pointer or keyboard targets; enter the child to inspect them. No deeper links or final payment routes are previewed. Hovering a current-level link fades in a thin 1.2-pixel accent over 180 ms, independently of its wider invisible hit target. Keyboard focus remains clearer; reduced motion removes the fade. Hiding a link affects presentation only. The Connections panel lists every current-level link and can reveal a hidden one.

Each drawn link terminates at actual plotted instances of its strongest shared identity. Selecting a shared object highlights its appearances in both groups and lists all corresponding source payments in paginated panels, including original IDs, amounts and UTC timestamps. Payment IDs locate editable payments in their final sector. These rows provide auditable graph evidence without displaying every label at once. Repeated object appearances preserve identity; payment membership remains unique across leaves. Position, distance and colour do not establish transaction chronology, geography or risk.

One screen-sized canvas stays mounted throughout group-to-group navigation. Geometry is computed once per layout, not at every zoom level. Canvas drawing follows the camera transform on animation frames; off-screen points and routes are culled, device-pixel ratio is capped at two, and no per-point DOM nodes or spring simulations run in overview. There is no deferred raster-resolution swap after zoom. No visible territory divisions are drawn; dot colours identify the current child sectors and blend when the focus changes. Neighbouring points remain as dim context. Sector numbers stay compact; full group names and grouping reasons are available in tooltips. Colours and the neutral background carry no risk meaning.

Navigation uses the same translated coordinates in the map and final interactive view. Final jelly objects resolve from small points at those positions. Returning converts the camera into parent coordinates before animating out. Reduced motion and the animation preference remove camera travel and object-resolution animation. Leaf edits update the point map without repositioning adjacent sectors. Deliberate zoom can navigate the existing hierarchy without changing its partitions. Multiple wheel events or zoom-button presses must accumulate at least a 1.35-fold magnification change in one direction. Entry additionally requires reaching 85% of the target sector's fitted magnification with the focal point inside it; exit requires dropping below 92% of the current sector's fitted magnification. A 120 ms settling delay, a short post-navigation cooldown and separate entry/exit thresholds prevent accidental single-scroll navigation and oscillation. A gesture can accumulate over up to four seconds between inputs. Large outward gestures catch up to the appropriate ancestor instead of leaving a distant map dimmed behind the current level. Automatic camera travel, fitting and panning never trigger hierarchy changes. Zoom-driven entry/exit rebases the current camera exactly, with no additional fit animation; final detail can zoom back to its parent but cannot subdivide further. Simulation always uses the full scenario, irrespective of the visible sector.

Limits are 10,000 source rows/payments, 1,000 objects, 20,000 associations and a 5 MB CSV. API JSON requests are bounded separately at 16 MiB. Sliding five-minute deques and account counters preserve inclusive window semantics, ASCII equal-time ordering, unavailable-rule exclusions and all attempted decisions. Evidence ID lists are capped at the latest 50 entries with `eventCount` disclosing the complete contributing population; measurements and risk points are never sampled. Replay preserves the pace of small scenarios and progressively compresses larger datasets. The presentation budget is max(30,000, 120,000 * sqrt(60 / max(60, payment count))) milliseconds; schedules shorter than that budget remain unchanged. The existing 1.25 clock factor makes the large-run floor about 24 seconds at 1x. A discrete slider offers 0.1x, 0.25x, 0.5x, 1x, 2x, 5x and 10x. This changes presentation only, never transaction timestamps, scoring windows or results. The Events list remains searchable and paginated, with every result available.
